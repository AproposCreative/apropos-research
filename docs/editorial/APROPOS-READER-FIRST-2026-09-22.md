# Reader-first editorial structure

Canonical rules: `prompts/structure.apropos.md` v4. Shared by all Writer authors,
Liv generation and the existing combined editorial assessment. Author voices remain
distinct. The Writer structure segment is mandatory, not a disableable style option.

Already present and retained: independent thesis, concrete evidence, meaningful
counterargument, dry humour, variation, research originality, selective attribution,
no fabricated firsthand experience, reasoned 1–6 ratings only for reviews, identifiable
review titles and the selected format's length policy. No extra research/model stage.

Added or reconciled:
- Reader understands event, actors, sequence, timeliness, relevance and article type
  before analysis; first 100–150 words and self-check after roughly 100 words.
- Human, clear subject-identifying headlines; subtitle now normally 10–25 factual
  words. Removed conflicting 8–14-word instructions in Writer and Liv.
- Continuous prose with normally one reflective closing heading and 2–4 short closing
  paragraphs within the existing total length; removed blanket bans on closing headings.
- A recognisable human observation and direct language instead of analysis announcements.
- Parties' accounts remain attributed; no invented motives.
- Exact review-only final sentence: “Læs Apropos Magazines anmeldelse her (X/6 stjerner).”
  X must match the reasoned rating; no invented link for “her”.
- Eleven self-edit questions before delivery. The existing editorial assessment sees
  these same criteria; it does not itself rewrite the article or buy new research.

Limits: semantic adherence is model-guided, not a claim of a deterministic 100-word
comprehension test. Existing blocking rules for facts, originality, attribution and
invented experience remain; minor stylistic feedback does not create an unlimited paid
rewrite loop. No existing draft or published article was rewritten by this change.
Regression uses simulated models and verifies prompt wiring and removal of conflicts;
it is not a claim that a new real article has passed human editorial review.
