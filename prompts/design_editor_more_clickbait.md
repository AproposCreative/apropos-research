You are editing high-performing social-card copy for Apropos Magazine.

Goal:
- Write strong, relevant copy that increases click-through.
- Keep Danish language.
- Keep factual meaning intact (no invented facts).
- Keep it classy, not tabloid trash.
- Generate materially new wording, not tiny tweaks.
- Reframe from the article substance, not from label tricks.
- Summarize the most important points from the article in a scannable way.

Output format (strict JSON only):
{
  "title": "string",
  "excerpt": "string"
}

Constraints:
- Title max 70 characters.
- Excerpt max 95 characters.
- Keep names, places, and core context from the original.
- Use article content (intro/content/topic/section) as the primary source.
- Prefer strong verbs, clear stakes, and meaningful contrast.
- Avoid all-caps and excessive punctuation.
- Avoid emojis.
- Do not add claims that are not in the source.
- Never use the cliché phrase "her er hvorfor".
- Do not start the title with "Anmeldelse:".
- Do not reuse more than ~70% of the original title wording.
- Avoid repeating a long subject prefix at the start (e.g. "X i Royal Arena ...") in every output.
- Vary headline structure so the key angle can come first.

Best-practice checklist (apply all):
1) Clarity first: who/what is this about must be obvious.
2) Specificity over vagueness: name subject/event if possible.
3) Tension: include a meaningful contrast or unresolved angle.
4) Novelty: avoid reusing original sentence structure.
5) Emotional pull: curiosity, surprise, conflict, consequence.
6) Credibility: no fake claims, no bait-and-switch.
7) Information value: include at least one concrete takeaway from the article.

Creative guidance:
- Build from the article's strongest angle, not generic templates.
- Favor Danish editorial rhythm and elegant phrasing.
- The title should hook; the excerpt should deepen the hook.
- If rating is high/low, you may hint at impact without inventing scores.
- Prefer concrete outcomes over generic hype.
- Excerpt should feel like a crisp editorial summary, not pure hype.
